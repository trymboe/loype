# Planning (`planner@1`)

Plans are generated by deterministic code in `src/lib/planning/` from your goal, your availability and a **baseline** (fitness estimate + what you actually ran in the last 4–8 weeks). No LLM is involved. Every workout stores *why* it is there.

## Baseline (`baseline.ts`)
- Weekly minutes, km and runs: average of the last 28 days.
- Consistency: weeks with at least one run, out of the last 8.
- Longest run: last 8 weeks.
- Fitness: VDOT, pace zones, HR zones and race predictions from the fitness model. Without an estimate, targets are HR-only; without HR zones, effort descriptions. Paces are never invented.

## Generator (`generator.ts`)
- **Days vs frequency**: the days you tick are *availability*, not a schedule, and there is no runs-per-week setting. Frequency is measured from your history, like volume: runs per week over the last 4 complete weeks (`recentRunCounts`, runs of 10 min or more) and your usual run length (`habitualRunMinutes`, the median over 8 weeks, 30 min if you have none). Each week's run count is its time divided by the run length, but the planner prefers one more run over longer runs while each run can still be at least 22 min: up to your habit plus one (at least 3) while you run under 4 a week, your habit after that. Floor 2; cap the larger of 1.3× your rolling 4-week average count and, under 4 a week, that average plus one (rolled forward with the planned weeks). So someone running 6 days a week gets about 6, someone running ~2 a week starts at 3, someone starting from nothing gets 2. As a week grows past where you started, runs lengthen as well (run length × √(growth), up to 60 min) so extra time is split between longer and more runs. Runs are spread around the week, keeping your long-run day; other days are rest days, and moving a session onto an unticked day warns you.
- **Weekly refresh**: at the first sync of each new week, the rest of the plan (this week onward) is recalculated from what you actually ran: last week, your 4-week average, how often you ran, and your longest run. If you handled more, it grows; if you did less, it backs off. Phases and the race date stay; locked, moved, swapped, finished and skipped workouts and accepted adaptations are kept; pending suggestions are retired; a version is saved, so it can be restored from History.
- **Length**: weeks to the race (max 24; if further away, the plan starts 24 weeks out), else the chosen length.
- **Phases**: taper weeks including race week: 5k/10k/half 2, marathon 3 (volume 75 % → 50 % of peak; marathon 80/60/45 %), with intensity and run frequency kept. Of the rest, ~30 % race-specific, ~35 % build, base first. A recovery week (70 %) every 4th week (every 3rd when consistency < 60 %), never just before the taper. A distance goal without a race date ends with a time trial.
- **Load limits (injury-aware)**: the first week starts from the larger of last week and your 4-week average, plus one full step (at least 15 min), and never above 1.3× the 4-week average (+10 min). Each week grows by at most 10 % (at least 10, at most 30 min; 6 %/6–20 min with patchy training), half that after a recovery week, and never above 1.3× the average of the 4 weeks before it (acute:chronic ratio). A week's planned sessions must stay within 10 % of its target: easy runs, then the long run, then hard sessions are shortened or dropped to fit. Peak caps by goal (5k 4 h, 10k 5 h, half 6 h, marathon 8 h) and your own limit.
- **Single-session spike limit**: no planned run (warm-up and cool-down included) is more than 10 % longer than the longest run in the 30 days before it: your real runs for the first month, then the plan's own sessions (a week's limit uses the sessions in the 24 days before it, which fall inside every day's 30-day window). Floor 30 min. A hard session that would break it is shortened, then replaced by an easy run. Easy and long runs are planned to the minute so they can grow in steps under 10 %.
- **Intensity (80/20)**: at most 20 % of a week's time at threshold or harder (minimum allowance 12 min); sessions are shortened, then dropped, to fit. Hard sessions: 2 with ≥ 4 runs, 1 with 3, none with 2; at most 1 in base weeks; none in the first two weeks when you run under 90 min a week. Hard sessions are ≥ 2 days from each other and the long run, and the day after one is a short recovery run.
- **Long run**: ~30 % of the week (more when you run 2–3 times), at least ~80 % of your recent longest run (bounded by 40 % of the week), grows at most 5 min a week, capped at 75/90/120/150 min (5k/10k/half/marathon).
- **Session types by phase**: base: tempo / hills + strides; build: threshold + VO2max (5k/10k/general) or threshold + tempo/VO2max (half/marathon); race-specific: race-pace + threshold/reps, long runs with goal-pace segments for half/marathon; taper: short race-pace + strides; race week: short easy runs (keeping your usual number of runs), a primer with strides, rest the day before.

## Evidence behind the rules

What each rule rests on, and how strong that is. Where the evidence is weak, the rule is a conservative convention, and it says so.

| Rule | Evidence | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No run > 10 % longer than your longest in the last 30 days | Frandsen et al., *Br J Sports Med* 2025;59:1203–10: 5,205 runners, ~588,000 Garmin-recorded sessions over 18 months. Runs >10–30 % longer than the 30-day longest had about 64 % higher injury rate (>100 %: about 2.3×). Weekly ratios (ACWR, week-to-week change) showed no clear association. | The best current evidence for running specifically. Observational, so it needs confirming. This is the primary safety rule here. |
| Weekly growth ≤ 10 %, ≤ 1.3× the 4-week average | Nielsen et al. 2014 (*JOSPT*): novices increasing >30 % over 2 weeks got more distance-related injuries. Buist et al. 2008 (*AJSM*): a 10 % schedule did not lower injuries versus faster progression. ACWR comes from team sports (Gabbett 2016) and is criticised statistically (Impellizzeri 2020). | Weak to moderate. Kept as a conservative secondary guard, not the main safety rule. |
| ~80 % easy, ≤ 20 % at threshold or harder | Seiler 2010 (intensity distribution in endurance athletes). Stöggl & Sperlich 2014. Muñoz et al. 2014 (*IJSPP*): recreational runners on a polarized ~80/20 split improved 10 km more than on a threshold-heavy split. | Moderate. Consistent across studies, and directly tested in recreational 10 km runners. |
| Session types per goal (VO2max 3–4 min reps, threshold cruise intervals, race-pace work, long runs) | Daniels' *Running Formula* (VDOT paces and zones). Helgerud et al. 2007 (4 × 4 min intervals raise VO2max). Training specificity: 5 km/10 km lean on VO2max and threshold work; the half on threshold; the marathon on long runs and marathon pace. | Moderate for the physiology (VO2max, lactate threshold, economy). The exact mix is coaching practice, not RCT-tested. |
| Base → build → race-specific → taper | Classical periodisation (Lydiard, Daniels' phases). | Coaching convention with good practical support; few controlled trials compare models for runners. |
| Taper ~2 weeks, volume −40–60 %, intensity and frequency kept | Bosquet et al. 2007 meta-analysis (*MSSE*): about 2 weeks, volume cut 41–60 %, no change to intensity or frequency. A 2023 endurance meta-analysis (*PLoS One*) found the same. | Strong (meta-analyses). |
| Recovery week every 3–4 weeks | Periodisation practice (load–recovery cycles). | Convention. Little direct trial evidence. |
| Race-time projection | VDOT gains per week by fitness level, diminishing returns, capped at 12 %. | Heuristic. Shown as a range and recalculated from real fitness. |

## Projection (`projection.ts`)
"If you follow this plan: 10 km X → Y". A heuristic, labelled as an estimate:
- Typical VDOT gain per training week by level: < 40: 0.30, < 45: 0.25, < 50: 0.20, < 55: 0.15, < 60: 0.10, else 0.06.
- Diminishing returns: effective weeks = 16 · (1 − e^(−weeks/16)).
- Volume factor: 0.7 + 0.3 · log2(plan average ÷ your recent weekly time), clamped to 0.5–1.2.
- Total gain capped at 12 % of VDOT. Range: 0.4× to 1.3× the gain. Endurance correction uses the longest planned long run.
- Updated on the Plan page from your current fitness estimate, the remaining training weeks and your completion rate over the last 4 weeks (0.5–1), and compared with the projection at the start.
- Race pace for race-specific sessions is your target pace when it is within the optimistic end of the projection; otherwise the projected pace, with a warning. Without a target, it is your current predicted pace (re-targeted by rule A as fitness changes).

## Matching and execution (`match.ts`)
- A run matches a planned workout on the same day (score 1) or ±1 day (0.6), minus a duration mismatch penalty, ± 0.1 for hard/easy agreement. Threshold 0.4; best pairs first. Unmatched runs stay unscheduled.
- A planned workout is **missed** once 2 days have passed without a match.
- Execution: work pace = your best effort whose duration is closest to one work bout, compared with the target range (±3 %); easy runs are checked against the easy HR ceiling; duration ratio < 75 % or > 130 % is noted.

## Adaptation (`adapt.ts`) — proposals only
| Rule | Trigger | Change |
|---|---|---|
| A | Fitness estimate moved ≥ 1 VDOT (medium/high confidence), or ≥ 2 of the last 3 quality sessions ≥ 3 % faster than target with RPE ≤ 7 | Re-target future paces (structure unchanged) |
| B | ≥ 2 missed sessions in 10 days and next week planned > 110 % of last 7 days' running + 30 min | Shorten non-key runs in the next 7 days; missed sessions are not made up |
| C | Easy run rated RPE ≥ 8 in the last 3 days, or legs heavy/sore in 2 of the last 3 check-ins | Next hard session within 3 days becomes an easy run |
| D | No run for ≥ 12 days | Next 7 days at ~55 %, no hard sessions |

Proposals are deduplicated by a key, expire when they would change the past, never touch locked or finished workouts, and can be accepted, declined ("Keep my plan") or undone. Changing the goal builds a new plan from today and archives the old one with its history.

## Versions
Every generation, edit, accepted proposal and undo writes a full snapshot to `plan_versions`. Any version can be restored from Plan → History (finished workouts are left as they are).

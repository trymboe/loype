# Planning (`planner@3`)

Plans are generated by deterministic code in `src/lib/planning/` from your goal, your availability and a **baseline** (fitness estimate + what you actually ran in the last 4–8 weeks). No LLM is involved. Every workout stores *why* it is there.

## Baseline (`baseline.ts`)
- Weekly minutes, km and runs: average of the last 28 days.
- Consistency: weeks with at least one run, out of the last 8.
- Longest run: last 8 weeks.
- Your runs (≥ 10 min) of the last 8 weeks, with date, minutes and km (`recentRuns`): session lengths and the spike limit start from them.
- Running load (`running-load@1`, `src/lib/analytics/running-load.ts`): each run in equivalent km = (km + ascent / 100 m) × intensity factor (1 + 0.5 × share of time in HR zone 4 + 1.0 × share in zone 5, from the fitness model's zone seconds; else from its HRR/pace intensity; else 1). Per complete week for the last 4 (`recentLoad`), and the last week ÷ the 4-week average (`loadRatio`).
- Fitness: VDOT, pace zones, HR zones and race predictions from the fitness model. Without an estimate, targets are HR-only; without HR zones, effort descriptions. Paces are never invented.

## Generator (`generator.ts`)
Sessions first, weekly volume second, and **the volume peaks and then holds**. A coach (or Runna) starts from the runs you actually do, builds each kind of run to a target for your race and chosen volume, and then keeps the week there while the sessions get more race-specific. (`planner@1` divided a weekly total over the runs, which gave a 4.7 km "long run"; `planner@2` seeded sessions from your runs but let the weekly total keep climbing toward a 5-hour cap and added runs over time, so a 10 km plan reached 32 km on 3 runs or 47 km on 5. `planner@3` fixes both.)

- **Options** (like Runna's Training Preferences), stored in the plan config:
  - **Runs per week**: a fixed number, or **Auto** = one more than you run now, at least 3 and at most 4 (more only if you already run more). One number for the whole plan; it never escalates.
  - **Training volume**: *Gradual* (7 %/week, a lighter week every 3rd week at 70 %), *Steady* (default; 10 %, every 4th at 75 %), *Progressive* (12 %, every 4th at 75 %). It also sets the peak long run and easy run (below).
  - **Session intensity**: *Comfortable* (one gentle quality session a week from 3 runs, none in base, no VO2max or fast reps, sessions at half length), *Balanced* (default; one a week, two with 4+ runs, none with 2 in base, sessions at 80 %), *Challenging* (two a week from 3 runs, full sessions).
- **Sizes are distance-based**: when your runs have distances and there is a pace model, every run (real or planned) is sized as its distance at your easy pace ("easy minutes"). Otherwise minutes.
- **Session peaks** (gradual / steady / progressive):
  - Long run, km: 5k 8/10/12, 10k 11/12/14, half 17/19/21, marathon 28/30/32; also capped in minutes (10k 75/85/95, half 120/135/150, marathon 150/165/180; other goals 60/75/90). If you already run longer, it holds at your level up to the time cap. *Maintain* holds your current long run.
  - Easy run, min: 35/40/50 for 5k/10k (half 40/45/55, marathon 45/55/65), never shorter than the easy runs you already do (up to 60). An easy run is at most 75 % of that week's long run.
  - Quality sessions use 10 + 5 min warm-up/cool-down for 5k/10k plans and small weeks, 15 + 10 otherwise.
- **Seeds**: long run = 80 % of the mean of your two longest runs in the last 6 weeks (at least 1.25× your usual run and 30 min), because the week around it is still small. Easy run = the median of your runs over 8 weeks.
- **Shape**: long and easy runs grow in a straight line from the seed to the peak over the loading weeks up to the end of the build phase (at most 10 loading weeks), then hold. The long run grows at most 10 % (or 5 min) over the last one and is at most 60 / 50 / 40 / 35 % of the week with 2 / 3 / 4 / 5+ runs (+5 points for the half, +10 for the marathon). In race-specific weeks every other long run is ~90 % with a faster finish (goal-pace segments for half/marathon). Lighter weeks: long run at 70–75 % of the last one. Taper: 65 % of the peak (marathon 75 % / 60 %); none in race week.
- **Weekly ceiling**: week 1 at most the larger of 1.3× your 4-week average + 10 min and your best week in the last 4 (90 % with patchy training; with next to no recorded running, ~22 min per run + 10). After a high-load week (running-load ratio > 1.3) only part of that growth over your 4-week average is allowed, none at ≥ 1.6 (`loadTemper`), so a short but hilly or hard week still counts as a big week; the weekly refresh applies this every week. Then a loading week grows at most the volume's rate (at least 6 min) over the last loading week, half that after a lighter week; lighter weeks 70–75 % of the last loading week; taper 75 % → 50 % of the peak week. Over the ceiling: easy runs shorten first, then hard sessions ease, then a hard session becomes easy (in a small week the long run matters more), then the long run (to no less than 90 % of its target). A loading week doesn't drop below your recent average.
- **Runs that don't fit yet**: if you choose more runs than the week has room for (each at least ~20 min next to a long run at 90 %), the plan starts with fewer and adds them as the week grows, and says so. Once added, a run stays; lighter and taper weeks keep the number of runs.
- **Weekly refresh**: at the first sync of each new week, the rest of the plan (this week onward) is recalculated from what you actually ran, including your recent long runs. Phases and the race date stay; locked, moved, swapped, finished and skipped workouts and accepted adaptations are kept; pending suggestions are retired; a version is saved, so it can be restored from History.
- **Length**: weeks to the race (max 24; if further away, the plan starts 24 weeks out unless you ask to start now), else the chosen length.
- **Phases**: taper weeks including race week: 5k/10k/half 2, marathon 3. Of the rest, ~30 % race-specific, ~35 % build, base first. A recovery week every 4th week (every 3rd when consistency < 60 %), never just before the taper. A distance goal without a race date ends with a time trial.
- **Single-session spike limit**: no planned run (warm-up and cool-down included) is more than 10 % bigger (by distance where known) than the biggest run in the 30 days before it: your real runs and the plan's own sessions from the 24 days before the week starts, which fall inside every day's 30-day window. Floor 30 min. A hard session that would break it is shortened, then replaced by an easy run.
- **Intensity (80/20)**: at most 20 % of a week's time at threshold or harder (30 % in the taper; minimum allowance 12 min); sessions are shortened, then dropped, to fit. No hard sessions in the first two weeks when you run under 90 min a week. Hard sessions are ≥ 2 days from each other and the long run, and the day after one is a short recovery run.
- **Long-run day**: optional. Set one and every long run goes there. Leave it on *Best fit* (the default) and the planner tries each day you can run with the week it would give and picks, in order: all the week's hard sessions ≥ 2 days from each other and the long run; the runs spread out; a rest day after the long run; no run (or at least no hard one) the day before. Ties go to the day of your longest run in the last 6 weeks, then the weekend. One day is kept for the whole plan, and it is picked again when the plan is recalculated, since the setting is stored as best fit.
- **Baseline time trial**: optional, on by default for a new plan (`baselineTimeTrial` in the config). Week 1 gets a time trial on a quality day ≥ 2 days from the long run and other hard sessions, in place of one hard session: 5 km, or 3 km for a 5 km goal or under 90 min of running a week, with warm-up and cool-down. It counts toward the week cap, the 80/20 allowance and the spike limit: it is shortened to 3 km (and a compact warm-up) to fit, kept until the long run is as short as it gets, and only then replaced by an easy run. Target: the time the fitness estimate predicts for the distance, else the time your best recent run (≥ 2 km, read as an all-out effort) predicts; no target without either. Only the plan's first week has one: a later refresh never adds it again.
- **Adding a time trial later** (Plan page button, or the coach's `add_time_trial`): one at a time, refused while an upcoming one exists. It replaces a planned run in the next 7 days (or on a chosen day) that isn't locked, finished, a race or a long run, ≥ 2 days from the long run and other hard sessions (a hard session preferred, so it adds no load). Built like the baseline time trial from the current fitness estimate; refused if even 3 km with a compact warm-up is over the spike limit. A hard session the day after becomes a recovery run. It is an athlete workout, so refreshes keep it and never add another; a recalculated week drops one hard session for it and keeps hard sessions off the days next to it.
- **Session types by phase** (race goals rotate through these, so each week differs): base: tempo, hills; build: threshold, VO2max (5k/10k) or threshold, tempo, threshold, VO2max (half/marathon); race-specific: race pace, threshold, race pace, fast repetitions (5k/10k) or race pace, threshold; taper: race pace. Comfortable drops VO2max and repetitions. Strides on easy days in lighter weeks and when there is a spare easy day. Race week: short easy runs, a primer with strides, rest the day before.

## Changing the goal or settings later
The Goal page edits the active plan. A new target time, race name or weekly-km goal is saved without touching any workout. A change to training settings (days, runs per week, usual frequency, training volume, session intensity, long-run day, max hours, session types) recalculates the rest of the plan from today with the weekly-update logic (`refreshFutureWeeks` with `force`): phases and race date stay, finished, skipped, locked and edited workouts are kept, and a version is recorded. Goal type, race date and length need a new plan (Delete plan, then create one).

## Evidence behind the rules

What each rule rests on, and how strong that is. Where the evidence is weak, the rule is a conservative convention, and it says so.

| Rule | Evidence | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No run > 10 % further than your furthest in the last 30 days | Frandsen et al., *Br J Sports Med* 2025;59:1203–10: 5,205 runners, ~588,000 Garmin-recorded sessions over 18 months. Single-session distance >10–30 % above the 30-day longest had about 64 % higher injury rate (>100 %: about 2.3×). Weekly ratios (ACWR, week-to-week change) showed no clear association. The study measured distance, so the planner does too where it can. | The best current evidence for running specifically. Observational, so it needs confirming. This is the primary safety rule here. |
| Long-run peak by race distance and volume (10 km: 11/12/14 km; half 17–21 km; marathon 28–32 km) | 5 and 10 km are ~90 % aerobic (Gastin 2001 energy-system contributions), so long runs past race distance build the endurance to hold pace late in the race; Daniels caps the long run at 150 min / 25–30 % of weekly volume; Pfitzinger & Latter's 5k–10k schedules use long runs well past race distance. Beyond ~90 min for a 10 km the recovery cost grows faster than the benefit. | Coaching practice built on solid physiology; the exact multiples are conventions. |
| Hold a new frequency/volume for a few weeks before the next step | Daniels: stay at a load for 3–4 weeks before increasing. | Convention. |
| Running load: 100 m climb ≈ 1 km; time in HR zones 4–5 weighted 1.5× / 2× per km; a load ratio > 1.3 holds week-1 growth back, ≥ 1.5 eases the next hard session | ITRA km-effort (km + ascent/100). Zone weights from Banister TRIMP per km. Ratios: Gabbett 2016, with the same caveats as below; Frandsen et al. 2025 found no clear weekly-ratio association, so load only ever holds the plan back and the single-run rule stays on distance. | Convention, conservative by design. |
| Weekly growth ≤ 10 %, week 1 ≤ 1.3× the 4-week average or your best recent week | Nielsen et al. 2014 (*JOSPT*): novices increasing >30 % over 2 weeks got more distance-related injuries. Buist et al. 2008 (*AJSM*): a 10 % schedule did not lower injuries versus faster progression. ACWR comes from team sports (Gabbett 2016) and is criticised statistically (Impellizzeri 2020). | Weak to moderate. Kept as a conservative secondary guard, not the main safety rule. |
| ~80 % easy, ≤ 20 % at threshold or harder | Seiler 2010 (intensity distribution in endurance athletes). Stöggl & Sperlich 2014. Muñoz et al. 2014 (*IJSPP*): recreational runners on a polarized ~80/20 split improved 10 km more than on a threshold-heavy split. | Moderate. Consistent across studies, and directly tested in recreational 10 km runners. |
| Session types per goal (VO2max 3–4 min reps, threshold cruise intervals, race-pace work, long runs) | Daniels' *Running Formula* (VDOT paces and zones). Helgerud et al. 2007 (4 × 4 min intervals raise VO2max). Training specificity: 5 km/10 km lean on VO2max and threshold work; the half on threshold; the marathon on long runs and marathon pace. | Moderate for the physiology (VO2max, lactate threshold, economy). The exact mix is coaching practice, not RCT-tested. |
| Base → build → race-specific → taper | Classical periodisation (Lydiard, Daniels' phases). | Coaching convention with good practical support; few controlled trials compare models for runners. |
| Taper ~2 weeks, volume −40–60 %, intensity and frequency kept | Bosquet et al. 2007 meta-analysis (*MSSE*): about 2 weeks, volume cut 41–60 %, no change to intensity or frequency. A 2023 endurance meta-analysis (*PLoS One*) found the same. | Strong (meta-analyses). |
| Recovery week every 3–4 weeks; volume peaks and then holds | Periodisation practice (load–recovery cycles); Runna's plans build "build, build, deload" toward a peak set by your ability, runs per week and volume preference, with a fixed ceiling on each week's increase (Runna help centre: *What is a build week*, *How does Runna build your plan*, *Training preferences*). Their sample 10 km plan (4 runs) peaks once and tapers. | Convention, shared by commercial plans. Little direct trial evidence. |
| Race-time projection | VDOT gains per week by fitness level, diminishing returns, capped at 12 %. | Heuristic. Shown as a range and recalculated from real fitness. |

## Projection (`projection.ts`)
"If you follow this plan: 10 km X → Y". A heuristic, labelled as an estimate:
- Typical VDOT gain per training week by level: < 40: 0.30, < 45: 0.25, < 50: 0.20, < 55: 0.15, < 60: 0.10, else 0.06.
- Diminishing returns: effective weeks = 16 · (1 − e^(−weeks/16)).
- Volume factor: 0.7 + 0.3 · log2(plan average ÷ your recent weekly time), clamped to 0.5–1.2.
- Total gain capped at 12 % of VDOT. Range: 0.4× to 1.3× the gain. Endurance correction uses the longest planned long run.
- Updated on the Plan page from your current fitness estimate, the remaining training weeks and your completion rate over the last 4 weeks (0.5–1), and compared with the projection at the start.
- Race pace for race-specific sessions is your target pace when it is within the optimistic end of the projection; otherwise the projected pace, with a warning. Without a target, it is your current predicted pace (re-targeted by rule A as fitness changes).

## Matching and execution (`match.ts`)
- A run matches a planned workout on the same day (score 1) or ±1 day (0.6), minus a duration mismatch penalty, ± 0.1 for hard/easy agreement. Threshold 0.4; best pairs first. Unmatched runs stay unscheduled.
- A planned workout is **missed** once 2 days have passed without a match.
- A run linked to a planned time trial is classified as a race (strong evidence for the fitness estimate) unless its intensity was under 80 % of HR reserve; execution records its target and actual time for the distance (best effort), shown on the workout page.
- Execution: work pace = your best effort whose duration is closest to one work bout, compared with the target range (±3 %); easy runs are checked against the easy HR ceiling; duration ratio < 75 % or > 130 % is noted.
- Rep by rep (`intervals.ts`, on the workout page): the main repeat's work bouts are taken from the watch laps when they match the plan (right length, faster than the laps around them), otherwise detected from the 15 s-smoothed speed stream with a threshold halfway between target and easy speed. Per rep: pace, avg HR, HR at the end and its drop over the next 60 s. Across reps: pace variation (CV; ≤ 2 % even, > 4 % uneven), fade (last third vs first third, ≥ 3 %), HR drift, and HR recovery shrinking below 70 % of the first rep's. Nothing is shown when the reps can't be found reliably.

## Editing a workout (`edit-steps.ts`)
Steps, reps, durations or distances, intensities and your own pace ranges can be edited. Targets are rebuilt from your zones (or your pace); a distance step gets an estimated time from its pace. Before saving, the edit is checked against the planner's rules (single-run spike limit against the longest run in the previous 30 days, real and planned; the week's hard share near 20 %; hard running added to an easy day; your own pace > 3 % faster than your current zone) and you can save anyway. The month view (Plan → Month) moves sessions by drag and drop with the same checks as moving from the workout page.

## Adaptation (`adapt.ts`) — proposals only
| Rule | Trigger | Change |
|---|---|---|
| A | Fitness estimate moved ≥ 1 VDOT (medium/high confidence), or ≥ 2 of the last 3 quality sessions ≥ 3 % faster than target with RPE ≤ 7 | Re-target future paces (structure unchanged) |
| B | ≥ 2 missed sessions in 10 days and next week planned > 110 % of last 7 days' running + 30 min | Shorten non-key runs in the next 7 days; missed sessions are not made up |
| C | Easy run rated RPE ≥ 8 in the last 3 days, legs heavy/sore in 2 of the last 3 check-ins, or wellness signals scoring ≥ 2 (`wellness-signals.ts`: 7-day ln HRV below your 60-day mean − 0.5 SD = 2; resting or sleeping HR ≥ 5 bpm and ≥ 1.5 SD above your mean on 2 of 3 days = 1; < 6 h sleep on 2 of 3 nights = 1; Body Battery peak < 40 on 2 of 3 days = 0.5; each metric needs 14 days of history), or running load of the last 7 days ≥ 1.5× the 28-day weekly average (runs in ≥ 3 of the 4 weeks) | Next hard session within 3 days becomes an easy run |
| D | No run for ≥ 12 days | Next 7 days at ~55 %, no hard sessions |

Proposals are deduplicated by a key, expire when they would change the past, never touch locked or finished workouts, and can be accepted, declined ("Keep my plan") or undone. "Explain further" shows what the rule looks at, why, the evidence behind it and what happens if you keep your plan. A proposal touching several workouts can be accepted for only the ticked ones; undo then reverts exactly those. Changing the goal builds a new plan from today and archives the old one with its history.

## Versions
Every generation, edit, accepted proposal and undo writes a full snapshot to `plan_versions`. Any version can be restored from Plan → History (finished workouts are left as they are).

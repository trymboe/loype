# Planning (`planner@2`)

Plans are generated by deterministic code in `src/lib/planning/` from your goal, your availability and a **baseline** (fitness estimate + what you actually ran in the last 4–8 weeks). No LLM is involved. Every workout stores *why* it is there.

## Baseline (`baseline.ts`)
- Weekly minutes, km and runs: average of the last 28 days.
- Consistency: weeks with at least one run, out of the last 8.
- Longest run: last 8 weeks.
- Your runs (≥ 10 min) of the last 8 weeks, with date, minutes and km (`recentRuns`): session lengths and the spike limit start from them.
- Fitness: VDOT, pace zones, HR zones and race predictions from the fitness model. Without an estimate, targets are HR-only; without HR zones, effort descriptions. Paces are never invented.

## Generator (`generator.ts`)
Sessions first, weekly time second. A coach starts from the runs you actually do: a long run like your recent long runs, easy runs like your usual run, quality sessions by phase. The week is the sum of those, kept under a weekly ceiling. (`planner@1` did it the other way round: it set a weekly total and divided it over the runs, which turned a runner with recent 8.7 km and 10 km runs into a 4.7 km "long run" next to a 3.5 km easy run.)

- **Sizes are distance-based**: when your runs have distances and there is a pace model, every run (real or planned) is sized as its distance at your easy pace ("easy minutes"). A fast 8.7 km counts as 8.7 km, not as its 45 minutes. Otherwise minutes.
- **Seeds**: long run = the larger of the mean of your two longest runs in the last 6 weeks and your longest in the last 2 weeks (at least 1.25× your usual run). Easy run = the median of your runs over 8 weeks (20–60 min). Shortest easy run ≈ 70 % of that (20–30 min).
- **Long-run shape**: from the seed, the long run grows evenly over the loading weeks to its peak at the end of the build phase, then holds through the race-specific phase (every other week ~90 % with a faster finish, or goal-pace segments for half/marathon). Recovery weeks 70 % of the last long run; taper 65 % of the peak (marathon 75 % / 60 %); none in race week.
- **Long-run peak by race**: the lesser of a time cap (5k 75, 10k 90, half 120, marathon 150 min; other goals 75–120) and a multiple of the race distance at your easy pace: 5k 2.4× (12 km), 10k 1.3× (13 km), half 1.1× (23 km), marathon 0.76× (32 km). If you already run longer than the peak, it holds at your level up to the time cap. *Maintain* holds your current long run.
- **Easy runs** grow with the same shape from your usual run to 55 % of the peak long run (max 60 min), and are never longer than 80 % of that week's long run.
- **Days vs frequency**: the days you tick are *availability*, not a schedule. Runs per week is **Auto** by default or a **fixed number** (1–7, never more than the ticked days; every week, recovery and taper too). Auto: the first week has at most one more run than your habit (at least 3, or a frequency you enter by hand with `usualRunsPerWeek`), then at most one more run every 3 loading weeks, up to 5 a week (6 for half/marathon) unless you already run more, and capped at 1.3× your rolling 4-week count. A run is only added when the week has room for it at a proper length (hard sessions counted as 1.25 easy runs, other runs as 0.85). Recovery weeks drop one run, tapers keep frequency. Runs are spread around the week, keeping your long-run day.
- **Weekly ceiling (load)**: week 1 at most the larger of 1.3× your 4-week average + 10 min and 120 % of your best week in the last 4 (110 % when consistency < 60 %). After that the ceiling rises one step a week (10 %, 10–30 min; 6 %/6–20 min with patchy training; half a step after a recovery week), and planned running never grows more than 15 % (or 15 min, or one added easy run) over the last loading week. Recovery weeks 75 % of the last loading week. Taper 75 % → 50 % of the peak week (marathon 80/60/45 %). Peak caps by goal (5k 4 h, 10k 5 h, half 6 h, marathon 8 h) and your own limit. Over the ceiling (+5 %): easy runs shorten first, then the long run, then hard sessions are eased and finally swapped for an easy run. Under your recent average, easy runs lengthen (up to 80 % of the long run).
- **Weekly refresh**: at the first sync of each new week, the rest of the plan (this week onward) is recalculated from what you actually ran, including your recent long runs. Phases and the race date stay; locked, moved, swapped, finished and skipped workouts and accepted adaptations are kept; pending suggestions are retired; a version is saved, so it can be restored from History.
- **Length**: weeks to the race (max 24; if further away, the plan starts 24 weeks out unless you ask to start now), else the chosen length.
- **Phases**: taper weeks including race week: 5k/10k/half 2, marathon 3. Of the rest, ~30 % race-specific, ~35 % build, base first. A recovery week every 4th week (every 3rd when consistency < 60 %), never just before the taper. A distance goal without a race date ends with a time trial.
- **Single-session spike limit**: no planned run (warm-up and cool-down included) is more than 10 % bigger (by distance where known) than the biggest run in the 30 days before it: your real runs and the plan's own sessions from the 24 days before the week starts, which fall inside every day's 30-day window. Floor 30 min. A hard session that would break it is shortened, then replaced by an easy run.
- **Intensity (80/20)**: at most 20 % of a week's time at threshold or harder (minimum allowance 12 min); sessions are shortened, then dropped, to fit. Hard sessions: 2 with ≥ 4 runs, 1 with 3, none with 2; at most 1 in base weeks; none in the first two weeks when you run under 90 min a week. With one hard session a week, the phase's two session types alternate. Hard sessions are ≥ 2 days from each other and the long run, and the day after one is a short recovery run.
- **Session types by phase**: base: tempo / hills + strides; build: VO2max + threshold (5k/10k/general) or threshold + tempo/VO2max (half/marathon); race-specific: race-pace + threshold/reps, long runs with goal-pace segments for half/marathon; taper: short race-pace + strides; race week: short easy runs (keeping your usual number of runs), a primer with strides, rest the day before.

## Changing the goal or settings later
The Goal page edits the active plan. A new target time, race name or weekly-km goal is saved without touching any workout. A change to training settings (days, runs per week, usual frequency, long-run day, max hours, session types) recalculates the rest of the plan from today with the weekly-update logic (`refreshFutureWeeks` with `force`): phases and race date stay, finished, skipped, locked and edited workouts are kept, and a version is recorded. Goal type, race date and length need a new plan (Delete plan, then create one).

## Evidence behind the rules

What each rule rests on, and how strong that is. Where the evidence is weak, the rule is a conservative convention, and it says so.

| Rule | Evidence | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No run > 10 % further than your furthest in the last 30 days | Frandsen et al., *Br J Sports Med* 2025;59:1203–10: 5,205 runners, ~588,000 Garmin-recorded sessions over 18 months. Single-session distance >10–30 % above the 30-day longest had about 64 % higher injury rate (>100 %: about 2.3×). Weekly ratios (ACWR, week-to-week change) showed no clear association. The study measured distance, so the planner does too where it can. | The best current evidence for running specifically. Observational, so it needs confirming. This is the primary safety rule here. |
| Long-run peak by race distance (5/10 km over-distance up to 2.4×/1.3×; half ~1.1×; marathon ≤ 32 km / 2.5 h) | 5 and 10 km are ~90 % aerobic (Gastin 2001 energy-system contributions), so long runs past race distance build the endurance to hold pace late in the race; Daniels caps the long run at 150 min / 25–30 % of weekly volume; Pfitzinger & Latter's 5k–10k schedules use long runs well past race distance. Beyond ~90 min for a 10 km the recovery cost grows faster than the benefit. | Coaching practice built on solid physiology; the exact multiples are conventions. |
| Hold a new frequency/volume for a few weeks before the next step | Daniels: stay at a load for 3–4 weeks before increasing. | Convention. |
| Weekly growth ≤ 10 % (≤ 15 % planned), week 1 ≤ 1.3× the 4-week average or 120 % of your best recent week | Nielsen et al. 2014 (*JOSPT*): novices increasing >30 % over 2 weeks got more distance-related injuries. Buist et al. 2008 (*AJSM*): a 10 % schedule did not lower injuries versus faster progression. ACWR comes from team sports (Gabbett 2016) and is criticised statistically (Impellizzeri 2020). | Weak to moderate. Kept as a conservative secondary guard, not the main safety rule. |
| ~80 % easy, ≤ 20 % at threshold or harder | Seiler 2010 (intensity distribution in endurance athletes). Stöggl & Sperlich 2014. Muñoz et al. 2014 (*IJSPP*): recreational runners on a polarized ~80/20 split improved 10 km more than on a threshold-heavy split. | Moderate. Consistent across studies, and directly tested in recreational 10 km runners. |
| Session types per goal (VO2max 3–4 min reps, threshold cruise intervals, race-pace work, long runs) | Daniels' *Running Formula* (VDOT paces and zones). Helgerud et al. 2007 (4 × 4 min intervals raise VO2max). Training specificity: 5 km/10 km lean on VO2max and threshold work; the half on threshold; the marathon on long runs and marathon pace. | Moderate for the physiology (VO2max, lactate threshold, economy). The exact mix is coaching practice, not RCT-tested. |
| Base → build → race-specific → taper | Classical periodisation (Lydiard, Daniels' phases). | Coaching convention with good practical support; few controlled trials compare models for runners. |
| Taper ~2 weeks, volume −40–60 %, intensity and frequency kept | Bosquet et al. 2007 meta-analysis (*MSSE*): about 2 weeks, volume cut 41–60 %, no change to intensity or frequency. A 2023 endurance meta-analysis (*PLoS One*) found the same. | Strong (meta-analyses). |
| Recovery week every 3–4 weeks | Periodisation practice (load–recovery cycles). | Convention. Little direct trial evidence. |
| Race-time projection | VDOT gains per week by fitness level, diminishing returns, capped at 12 %. | Heuristic. Shown as a range and recalculated from real fitness. |

## Projection (`projection.ts`)
"If you follow this plan: 10 km X → Y". A heuristic, labelled as an estimate:
- Typical VDOT gain per training week by level: < 40: 0.30, < 45: 0.25, < 50: 0.20, < 55: 0.15, < 60: 0.10, else 0.06.
- Diminishing returns: effective weeks = 16 · (1 − e^(−weeks/16)).
- Volume factor: 0.7 + 0.3 · log2(plan average ÷ your recent weekly time), clamped to 0.5–1.2.
- Total gain capped at 12 % of VDOT. Range: 0.4× to 1.3× the gain. Endurance correction uses the longest planned long run.
- Updated on the Plan page from your current fitness estimate, the remaining training weeks and your completion rate over the last 4 weeks (0.5–1), and compared with the projection at the start.
- Race pace for race-specific sessions is your target pace when it is within the optimistic end of the projection; otherwise the projected pace, with a warning. Without a target, it is your current predicted pace (re-targeted by rule A as fitness changes).

## Matching and execution (`match.ts`)
- A run matches a planned workout on the same day (score 1) or ±1 day (0.6), minus a duration mismatch penalty, ± 0.1 for hard/easy agreement. Threshold 0.4; best pairs first. Unmatched runs stay unscheduled.
- A planned workout is **missed** once 2 days have passed without a match.
- Execution: work pace = your best effort whose duration is closest to one work bout, compared with the target range (±3 %); easy runs are checked against the easy HR ceiling; duration ratio < 75 % or > 130 % is noted.
- Rep by rep (`intervals.ts`, on the workout page): the main repeat's work bouts are taken from the watch laps when they match the plan (right length, faster than the laps around them), otherwise detected from the 15 s-smoothed speed stream with a threshold halfway between target and easy speed. Per rep: pace, avg HR, HR at the end and its drop over the next 60 s. Across reps: pace variation (CV; ≤ 2 % even, > 4 % uneven), fade (last third vs first third, ≥ 3 %), HR drift, and HR recovery shrinking below 70 % of the first rep's. Nothing is shown when the reps can't be found reliably.

## Editing a workout (`edit-steps.ts`)
Steps, reps, durations or distances, intensities and your own pace ranges can be edited. Targets are rebuilt from your zones (or your pace); a distance step gets an estimated time from its pace. Before saving, the edit is checked against the planner's rules (single-run spike limit against the longest run in the previous 30 days, real and planned; the week's hard share near 20 %; hard running added to an easy day; your own pace > 3 % faster than your current zone) and you can save anyway. The month view (Plan → Month) moves sessions by drag and drop with the same checks as moving from the workout page.

## Adaptation (`adapt.ts`) — proposals only
| Rule | Trigger | Change |
|---|---|---|
| A | Fitness estimate moved ≥ 1 VDOT (medium/high confidence), or ≥ 2 of the last 3 quality sessions ≥ 3 % faster than target with RPE ≤ 7 | Re-target future paces (structure unchanged) |
| B | ≥ 2 missed sessions in 10 days and next week planned > 110 % of last 7 days' running + 30 min | Shorten non-key runs in the next 7 days; missed sessions are not made up |
| C | Easy run rated RPE ≥ 8 in the last 3 days, legs heavy/sore in 2 of the last 3 check-ins, or wellness signals scoring ≥ 2 (`wellness-signals.ts`: 7-day ln HRV below your 60-day mean − 0.5 SD = 2; resting or sleeping HR ≥ 5 bpm and ≥ 1.5 SD above your mean on 2 of 3 days = 1; < 6 h sleep on 2 of 3 nights = 1; Body Battery peak < 40 on 2 of 3 days = 0.5; each metric needs 14 days of history) | Next hard session within 3 days becomes an easy run |
| D | No run for ≥ 12 days | Next 7 days at ~55 %, no hard sessions |

Proposals are deduplicated by a key, expire when they would change the past, never touch locked or finished workouts, and can be accepted, declined ("Keep my plan") or undone. "Explain further" shows what the rule looks at, why, the evidence behind it and what happens if you keep your plan. A proposal touching several workouts can be accepted for only the ticked ones; undo then reverts exactly those. Changing the goal builds a new plan from today and archives the old one with its history.

## Versions
Every generation, edit, accepted proposal and undo writes a full snapshot to `plan_versions`. Any version can be restored from Plan → History (finished workouts are left as they are).
